FAST NUCES

XAIHate: explainable hate-speech detection for Urdu.

A bachelor’s thesis combining multilingual classification with word-level explanations.

Bachelor’s thesis · Team: Maria Yasir, Maaz Asad & Rizwan Haidar

87.7% Urdu accuracy
TF-IDFNaive BayesRandom ForestBERTDistilBERTLIMEFlask

IN THIS STUDY

Research questionApproach & modelsEvaluation & resultsLimitationsAttribution & next questions
01

Research question

How can hate-speech detection in Urdu, Roman Urdu and English be paired with explanations that communicate why a text received its label?

02

Approach & models

The team compared TF-IDF-based Naive Bayes, logistic regression and random forests with BERT, DistilBERT and RoBERTa. A perturbation-based explainer estimated word contributions and compared important features against LIME. A Flask application presented highlighted text, interactive contribution plots and word clouds.

03

Evaluation & results

The report evaluates classification with accuracy and, for deep learning, precision, recall and F1. DistilBERT reached 87.7% accuracy and 0.81 F1 on Urdu, and 88.05% accuracy on Roman Urdu. BERT reached 96.11% accuracy and 0.94 F1 on English. These are team-reported results on separate language datasets, not directly comparable language benchmarks.

04

Limitations

Explanation validation was carried out for the classical machine-learning models; deep-learning explanations were constrained by computation. Agreement with LIME is a comparison against another explanation method, not proof of causal faithfulness. The report also discusses the trade-off between inference speed and predictive performance.

05

Attribution & next questions

Co-authored by Maria Yasir, Maaz Asad and Rizwan Haidar, supervised by Umair Arshad with Omer Beg as co-supervisor. The report does not provide an individual task breakdown. Future work includes extending explainability to deep-learning models and improving annotation and preprocessing.

SOURCE & ATTRIBUTION
