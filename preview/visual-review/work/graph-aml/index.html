01

Research question

Which graph architectural choices improve edge-level money-laundering detection under extreme class imbalance, and how reproducible are the gains?

02

My contribution

Reproduced Multi-GIN + EU experiments and implemented the FRAD-B edge-feature variant on that baseline. Wrote the introduction and problem statement and contributed the Multi-GNN / FRAD-DM theory, experimental setup and results sections. The FRAD-DM-inspired reconstruction is a controlled partial variant, not a reproduction of the full original pipeline.

03

Team & evaluation

Team: Avin Safari Shirzi, Mohamed Abouelella, Rizwan Haidar and Haofan Wen. The study uses the synthetic AMLworld benchmark, treating transactions as directed multigraph edges. Evaluation selects the best-validation checkpoint and reports minority-class F1 across random seeds. Other team members implemented D-EMAML, FraudGT and C2GAT.

04

Results & interpretation

On Small-HI, Table 3 reports minority-class F1 of 59.9 ± 7.1 for reproduced Multi-GIN + EU and 54.07 ± 10.7 for FRAD-B. The variant did not establish an improvement; the gap is smaller than the seed-wise variability. The team’s strongest FraudGT extension reached 77.6 ± 1.5, versus 76.4 ± 1.5 for its reproduced baseline; this result belongs to the collaborative study, not my individual implementation.

05

Limitations & future work

Approaches differ in preprocessing, graph coverage and training setup, so a simple cross-model ranking would overstate comparability. Data is synthetic; D-EMAML uses pruned coverage. Further controlled ablations and more consistent preprocessing are needed before drawing broader conclusions.
