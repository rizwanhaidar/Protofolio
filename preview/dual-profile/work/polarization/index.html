Polarization detection across English and Urdu.

Supervised multilingual models and in-context learning under linguistic and label-distribution shift.

IN THIS STUDY

Research questionTeam & methodsData & evaluationResultsLimitations & future work
01

Research question

How do supervised multilingual transformers and prompted LLMs compare for multilabel polarization classification when English and Urdu differ strongly in language and label distributions?

02

Team & methods

Collaborative report by Maaz Asad, Maria Yasir, Aqib Amir Khan and Rizwan Haidar (spelled “haider” on the report cover). RemBERT and XLM-RoBERTa were evaluated in monolingual, joint multilingual and cross-lingual settings, with per-label threshold tuning. GPT-OSS, LLaMA and Qwen variants were tested through zero- and few-shot prompting.

03

Data & evaluation

POLAR@SemEval-2026 Task 9, Subtask 2 assigns five polarization categories: political, racial/ethnic, religious, gender/sexual and other. Macro-F1 is the primary metric because label frequencies are imbalanced. English has sparse labels; Urdu has denser, frequently overlapping labels.

04

Results

Table 2 reports monolingual RemBERT macro-F1 of 0.4615 for English and 0.7630 for Urdu, compared with 0.3608 and 0.7400 for XLM-RoBERTa. XLM-R cross-lingual zero-shot scores fell to 0.2018 and 0.2991. Joint training reduced English performance. Prompting benefits varied across models and languages.

05

Limitations & future work

Language, culture and label distribution are entangled, limiting cross-lingual conclusions. Model and prompt coverage is limited; the report does not establish a competition ranking. Future work includes English data augmentation and more robust cross-lingual adaptation. Individual task ownership is not specified in the report.

SOURCE & ATTRIBUTION

Team 25 NLP project report, dated February 9, 2026, sections 3–9 and Tables 2–3.
