Natural language inference beyond benchmark accuracy.

Comparing pretraining, shortcut learning and cross-genre generalization on SNLI and MultiNLI.

Master’s project · Mining Media Data 2 · Team 3

IN THIS STUDY

Research questionTeam & approachData & evaluationResultsLimitations & future work
01

Research question

How much does large-scale pretraining contribute to natural language inference, and do high benchmark scores survive domain shift and shortcut-focused diagnostics?

02

Team & approach

Collaborative work by Maaz Asad, Maria Yasir and Rizwan Haidar. The team compared a three-layer, six-head Transformer initialized with 300-dimensional GloVe vectors against BERT-base-uncased and DeBERTa-v3-base. The report does not assign individual implementation ownership.

03

Data & evaluation

After cleaning SNLI, the study used 549,361 training, 9,842 validation and 9,824 test pairs. Models classify entailment, contradiction and neutral. Evaluation includes held-out accuracy, per-class recall, MultiNLI transfer and diagnostic subsets for overlap, negation and length mismatch.

04

Results

SNLI test accuracy was 91.04% for BERT, 90.30% for DeBERTa and 80.84% for the custom Transformer. On combined MultiNLI validation data, accuracy fell to 74.31%, 70.83% and 44.91%, respectively. High in-domain performance therefore did not establish robust cross-genre reasoning.

05

Limitations & future work

Training used one image-caption domain. The custom model had not converged at epoch 25, and small diagnostic subsets limit strong conclusions. Future evaluation on HANS or SNLI-hard, MultiNLI fine-tuning and adversarial augmentation would better test generalization.
