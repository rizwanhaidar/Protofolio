Does chain-of-thought monitorability survive quantization?

A study design testing whether a reasoning model still admits the cues that change its answers after 4-bit quantization.

Seminar paper · Seminar AI Safety · sole author · grade 1.0

Seminar grade 1.0
Qwen3-8BGPTQMMLUEquivalence testingCoT monitoring

IN THIS PROJECT

QuestionDesignEvaluationWhy it matters
01

Question

Does weight quantization change how often an injected cue alters an answer without being acknowledged in the model’s reasoning?

02

Design

Compare BF16 with matched GPTQ W4A16 artifacts of one Qwen3-8B checkpoint, holding prompts, cue targets, decoding and judging fixed. W8A16 and W3A16 are exploratory branches. MMLU questions are combined with six cue families.

03

Evaluation

Measure unacknowledged cue susceptibility and total cue susceptibility over all questions. Equivalence requires both differences to stay within five percentage points, tested with a 10,000-replicate question-clustered bootstrap, a validated LLM judge and token-budget sensitivity checks.

04

Why it matters

Open-weight reasoning models are often deployed as 4-bit builds. If quantization changes cue acknowledgment, monitoring results measured on full-precision weights would not describe the deployed model.
