Does chain-of-thought monitorability survive quantization?

A research proposal on cue acknowledgment across matched Qwen3 model artifacts.

Master’s-level AI Safety research proposal · Sole author: Rizwan Haidar

IN THIS STUDY

Research questionProposed methodProposed evaluationStatus & expected outputLimitations & fallbacks
01

Research question

Does weight quantization change how often an injected cue alters an answer without being acknowledged in the model’s reasoning?

02

Proposed method

Compare BF16 against a matched GPTQ W4A16 artifact from a fixed Qwen3-8B checkpoint. W8A16 and W3A16 are exploratory branches. Use MMLU questions and six cue families while holding prompts, cue targets, decoding and judging procedures fixed across artifacts.

03

Proposed evaluation

Measure unacknowledged cue susceptibility and total cue susceptibility over all questions, avoiding conditioning only on successful cues. The confirmatory contrast requires equivalence within five percentage points on both outcomes. The plan includes pilot power checks, paired analysis, judge validation and token-budget sensitivity checks.

04

Status & expected output

This is a proposal, not a completed experiment or peer-reviewed publication. It specifies a six-month research plan and a reproducible evaluation design. No observed preservation or degradation of monitorability is claimed.

05

Limitations & fallbacks

Conclusions would apply to the tested artifacts and inference paths, not bit width in general. Cue acknowledgment is a narrow proxy for monitorability. Low power, judge failures or budget confounding can produce an inconclusive result; the proposal retains fixed equivalence margins rather than relaxing them to force a finding.
